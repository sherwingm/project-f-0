# Final findings: market conditions behind G1-F and the other index strategies (17 Nov 2026)

Prepared by the coordinator from five independent agents' work (inventory, trade anatomy, regimes, GIFT overnight regimes, institutional / global / news) and an independent statistical audit. Details: `DATA_INVENTORY.md`, `G1F_TRADE_ANATOMY.md`, `MARKET_REGIME_RESULTS.md`, `STRATEGY_REGIME_COMPARISON.md`, `PREDICTIVE_VALIDATION.md`, `RESEARCH_LIMITATIONS.md`, `results_tables/REGIME_STATS_TABLES.md`. This is a research investigation, not an optimisation: **no strategy rule, parameter or live system was changed or recommended for change; no switching system was built; no winning strategy is declared.** Original recorded trade results only (G1-F 154, G1 259, G1-C 235, XP 107; entries 2024-10-01 to 2026-10-28).

## The short answer
In these two years **no market condition known before entry explains G1-F's good and poor periods with statistical support.** About 37 pre-registered tests per strategy gave 4 nominal hits for G1-F against 1.8 expected by chance, **none survived the multiple-comparison correction** (smallest q 0.37), and none of the nominal hits is consistent across the two years. What clearly separates G1-F's winning trades from its losing trades is **not known at entry**: it is the next session's market move. This is "no evidence found at these sample sizes", **not** evidence that conditions do not matter: effects below about 45 points are invisible in cells of 80 trades.

## Answers to the 11 questions
1. **What distinguishes G1-F's profitable from losing trades?** Post-entry: 90 % of winners had the next close below the entry-day close vs 40 % of losers (Spearman of net % with the signal-to-next-close spot move -0.74); winners are the 70 %-winning trades held to the next close, and 77 % of the rupees come from 8 trades, all held overnight into a 0.4 % to 2.9 % fall. Pre-entry, 11 of 13 fields (returns, VIX, gap, intraday move, breakout minute, breadth, IV, put-call OI) are the same for winners and losers; only sessions to expiry (3.25 vs 2.79) and price paid (127 vs 112) differ, at uncorrected p 0.048 and 0.018 from about 25 comparisons. Stops are slow (median 309 minutes, 42 % of trades) and the stop rate falls from 71 % at one session to expiry to 26 % at four or more.
2. **Does market direction have a meaningful relationship with G1-F's performance?** No supported relationship (Kruskal-Wallis p 0.51). Bullish n 19 +59.5 % (+14.6 % without its top 3 trades) vs bearish n 75 +11.7 % and sideways n 60 +16.9 %. For G1 puts, bearish regimes were not better (-0.8 % vs +22.6 % in bullish); for G1-C calls, bullish regimes were much worse (-36 %, n 41; a lead).
3. **Does India VIX explain differences?** Only nominally: normal-VIX trades did better than low-VIX (+38.1 % vs +11.8 %; KW p 0.020, q 0.37). It is +5 points in discovery, comes from 8 later trades, is confounded with calendar time, reverses in 2024 H2, and G1-F has no high-VIX trades. Rising / falling VIX and expansion: nothing (p 0.95; 2 trades).
4. **Does GIFT NIFTY provide useful pre-entry information?** It tracks the actual opening gap (correlation 0.75, same-contract) so it does carry overnight information, but **its gap class does not separate G1-F outcomes** (gap-down 28 +26.7 %, flat 111 +19.4 %, gap-up 13 +17.5 %; p 0.947). The indicated-vs-actual comparison is a lead (cash open "in line" with GIFT -38 points; same sign in both years; p 0.077; q 0.49). The 09:00 GIFT level, the most natural pre-open indication, does not exist in the data.
5. **Do FII/DII flows add information?** Cash flows exist only for Dec 2025 to Oct 2026 (59 G1-F trades; third-party; September 2026 missing): both buying n 15 -29.4 % (one winner) vs diverging n 44 +34.3 % (Welch p 0.004, KW p 0.082): descriptive and window-limited. Participant-wise futures and options positioning (all dates): nothing (p at least 0.10).
6. **Do global markets provide additional information?** No. Asia (p 0.58 / 0.85), US overnight (0.26), risk-on / off (0.78), Brent (0.84, quality-limited), dollar index (0.93), US 10-year (0.27). USD/INR has one nominal p of 0.042 (large moves either way better than flat), 1 hit in 19 tests, which is what chance produces. US VIX spike: G1-F +0.3 % vs +19.7 % (second year -29 %); for G1-C calls the opposite (+25.9 % vs -15.3 %, same direction in both years, n 23): a hypothesis only.
7. **Do news and economic events matter?** Not detectable: scheduled-event days n 12 (-1.4 %), day before n 6 and day after n 8 (too few); GDELT tone (global, daily) p 0.70. No timestamped news or other calendar dates exist, so reaction windows (5 to 60 minutes) and unexpected news could not be tested.
8. **Are combinations more informative than single variables?** No. The only combination hit (VIX level x gap, p 0.039) is the VIX-level effect again; direction x GIFT p 0.74; VIX x GIFT (faithful definition) p 0.164; the triple is insufficient for every strategy (cells under 10 trades).
9. **Are any relationships consistent across periods?** None strongly. Same sign in both years but not significant: indicated-vs-actual "in line" worse; breadth mid above low; flat USD/INR weakest; the 5-day-return middle quintile positive in all four testable half-years; G1-C weak after bullish run-ups (-33 / -28 points); G1-C better after a US VIX spike. Reversed: Asia, high breadth, sector leadership, the VIX-level gap, XP's VIX direction. Half-year results swing widely (2026 H1 +64 %, 2026 H2 -5.6 %) whatever the regime.
10. **Is there evidence of genuine predictive value rather than historical correlation?** No. Nothing passed the correction in discovery for G1-F (0 of 31 tests below 0.05 in discovery), nothing passed out-of-sample validation, and the second half is not even a clean holdout.
11. **What is still impossible to establish?** The 09:00 GIFT level and its relation to the open; FII / DII cash effects over a full history (data before Dec 2025 and for Sep 2026 missing); news and announcement-reaction effects (no timestamped news); high-VIX behaviour of G1-F (none by its filter); gap-up behaviour (filtered out); the strongest bullish regimes (6 trades); any effect smaller than about 45 points; whether conditions matter for BANKNIFTY or SENSEX versions (no trade records); anything on trending vs range-bound sessions, intraday reversals and failed breakouts defined before entry (not built); behaviour outside this two-year, mostly low-volatility phase.

## Classification
**Supported by evidence**
- G1-F's outcome is determined after entry by the next session's market move; profit is concentrated in a few overnight winners (top 8 trades = 77 % of rupees; mean +5.8 % without them) and the strategy pays for them with many slow-bleeding stops (42 % of trades). The recorded trades reproduce (154 of 154).
- Pre-entry fields available in the study do not separate winners from losers in the aggregate (descriptively consistent across G1, G1-F and G1-C, which are not independent).
- GIFT's 06:30 open (same-contract basis) tracks the actual NIFTY opening gap (r 0.75), whereas the `prev_settle` column does not (r 0.50): the choice of definition matters.
- With about 37 tests per strategy, 4 nominal hits for G1-F is within what chance gives (1.8 expected; P(at least 4) = 0.10), and none survives the correction.
**Interesting but unproven (leads; each small, partly period-confounded, none corrected-significant)**
- Cash open "in line" with GIFT does worse than days when it diverges (G1-F; same sign in both years).
- Normal-VIX days do better than low-VIX days (G1-F); no high-VIX data.
- FII / DII both buying (poor) vs diverging (good) in the Dec 2025 to Oct 2026 window.
- G1-C calls after bullish run-ups perform very badly; G1-C calls do better after a US VIX spike; G1-F's results are better after large USD/INR moves.
- Mid breadth better than low or high breadth; the 5-day-return middle quintile.
- Stop rate falls as sessions to expiry rise (descriptive; net returns by that cut are not different).
**Not supported by evidence**
- Market direction (five-way and three-way), VIX direction and expansion, actual opening gap, GIFT gap class, Asia pre-entry, global risk-on / off, S&P overnight, Brent, dollar index, US 10-year, sector leadership, news tone, scheduled-event windows, participant-wise positioning, and any combination of them, as explanations of G1-F's periods.
- That puts do better in bearish regimes (G1 puts: bearish -0.8 %) or calls in bullish regimes (G1-C calls: bullish -36 %).
- XP's discovery-period VIX-direction pattern (reversed in the second year).

## What this means for the original question
"Why does G1-F do well in some periods and poorly in others?" The best-supported answer is mechanical, not environmental: **the result is a few large overnight winners (next-session falls of about 0.4 % to 2.9 %) against many stop-outs, so a period does well when such falls occur and poorly when they do not (2026 Q3: next close +0.03 % vs -0.21 % in other quarters) and nothing observable before entry was found that foretells them.** Whether any market condition does is unknown at this sample size and phase, and this study cannot rule it in or out.
